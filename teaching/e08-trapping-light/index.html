E08 / WHY LEARN THIS?

How do you route and store light on a chip?

An optical circuit must keep light inside a guide, transfer it to a neighbouring path, or build it up in a resonator. Drawing the paths is not enough to predict the power: the supported field shapes and their relative phases determine what the device does.

What you gain: Connect confinement to guided modes, choose a coupling length from mode beating, and explain how round-trip phase, coupling, and loss set a ring's spectral response.

Unpack the difficult steps

Why are there only certain modes if many rays satisfy total internal reflection?

0:49 · Replay this part

2kx d + φr,1 + φr,2 = 2πm
[d²/dx² + k₀²n²(x)] ψ = β²ψ

Confinement alone is insufficient: the field must also reproduce itself after transverse propagation and reflection. Reflection phases at both interfaces belong in the round-trip condition. The differential eigenproblem imposes the same self-consistency through boundary matching and decaying cladding fields.

For a bound mode of a simple lossless step-index slab, nclad k₀ < β < ncore k₀. In the scalar matrix written above, the eigenvalue is β²; take the appropriate positive square root for forward propagation. Not every numerical eigenvector is a guided mode: finite windows also discretize radiation states.

Is light gradually leaking between guides, or is it mode interference?

2:15 · Replay this part

β± = β₀ ± κ
a₁(z) = e^(iβ₀z) cos(κz), a₂(z) = i e^(iβ₀z) sin(κz)
P₂/Ptotal = sin²(κz), Ltransfer = π/(2κ)

In an ideal identical, lossless pair, exciting one guide is an equal superposition of the symmetric and antisymmetric supermodes. Their propagation-constant difference is 2κ. After π/(2κ), their relative phase has changed by π: interference cancels in the launch guide and reinforces in the other.

The evanescent overlap sets κ, while interference describes the resulting power exchange. A 50:50 split occurs at π/(4κ). Unequal guide propagation constants reduce the maximum transfer; the ideal complete-transfer formula should not be applied to a detuned pair without modification.

Why does a long storage time mean a narrow resonance?

3:25 · Replay this part

energy: W(t) ∝ e^(−t/τE)
field: a(t) ∝ e^(−t/(2τE)) e^(−iω₀t)
ΔωFWHM = 1/τE, Q = ω₀τE = ω₀/ΔωFWHM

A slowly decaying field retains a definite oscillation for longer, so its Fourier response is concentrated in a narrower frequency range. Fourier-transforming the exponential amplitude decay gives a Lorentzian power resonance. The factor of two between amplitude and energy decay is essential.

For an isolated narrow resonance, Q ≈ λ₀/Δλ. Its loaded lifetime counts all energy escape channels, including useful extraction into a waveguide. Thus increasing coupling can broaden a resonance even when the material absorption has not changed. Free spectral range, the spacing between resonances, is a different quantity from linewidth.

If the through port goes dark, where does the energy go?

3:41 · Replay this part

single-bus, on resonance: t = (γi − γe)/(γi + γe)
critical coupling: γe = γi

Here γi and γe are internal and external amplitude-decay rates in a consistent coupled-mode convention. At critical coupling, the direct and ring-leaked fields cancel at the through port. In a single-bus lossy ring, the missing steady-state power is dissipated internally. During turn-on, some power first builds stored energy.

The opening add-drop ring has a second bus. That bus supplies another escape channel, so cancellation at the input bus requires its coupling rate to match the sum of intrinsic loss and coupling to the drop bus. Power can then emerge from the drop port. A lossless single-bus ring is instead an all-pass device in steady state; it does not absorb all input power on resonance.

01 / BUILD AN INTUITION

What to notice first.

A slab waveguide has a high-index core surrounded by a lower-index cladding. Total internal reflection suggests that light can remain near the core, but not every zigzag angle gives a self-consistent wave. The transverse field must match at both interfaces and decay into the cladding. Those conditions select discrete modes.

Write a mode as E(x,z) = ψ(x)eiβz. The transverse shape ψ stays fixed while propagation adds a phase. Substituting this form into the scalar wave equation produces an eigenvalue problem.

[d²/dx² + k₀²n²(x)]ψ = β²ψ   ·   n_clad < β/k₀ < n_core

The ratio β/k₀ is the effective index. The inequalities identify guided modes in this lossless slab. A mode’s tail is part of its field, not an error to trim away. Near cutoff, the tail can extend far into the cladding, so a small simulation window changes the answer.

Any suitable input can be expanded into supported modes. Their individual shapes do not change, but their relative phases do. Interference between them can move the total intensity from one part of the guide to another. This is the guided-wave version of changing basis before propagation.

By the end, you can

  1. Recognize a guided mode as an eigenfunction with decaying cladding tails.
  2. Explain directional coupling through relative supermode phase.
  3. Relate ring resonance, storage, and critical coupling without confusing amplitude and power.

03 / CONNECT THE LOGIC

Power moves because phases separate.

Two nearby identical guides support symmetric and antisymmetric supermodes with slightly different propagation constants. An input concentrated in one guide is their superposition. If the constants differ by 2κ, their relative phase reaches π after L = π/(2κ), transferring the intensity to the other guide.

P₁(z) = cos²(κz)   ·   P₂(z) = sin²(κz)

The browser treats κ as a specified parameter of an ideal, lossless two-mode model. The notebook computes it from the eigenvalue splitting of a double-guide index profile. Unequal guides, radiation, additional modes, or loss can prevent complete transfer.

A resonator adds a field to delayed copies of itself.

After one round trip, a circulating field is multiplied by ra eiφ, where r is the coupler’s self-coupling amplitude, a is the propagation survival amplitude, and φ is round-trip phase. Summing all returns gives a geometric series proportional to 1/(1 − ra eiφ). On resonance, the returns reinforce each other.

The circulating power can exceed input power because energy remains for many trips. This is storage, not gain. Once the drive stops, the stored energy decreases by (ra)² per round trip. The decay time and spectral linewidth express the same finite lifetime. An absolute quality factor also needs the optical frequency and round-trip time, so the page reports round trips rather than inventing a device Q.

t_through = (r − a eiφ)/(1 − ra eiφ)

At resonance, the through port vanishes when r = a. This is critical coupling: direct transmission interferes destructively with the field leaking back from the ring. In a lossy all-pass ring, the missing through power is dissipated internally; zero transmission is not proof of perfect reflection. If a = 1, the lossless all-pass ring has unit through power away from singular limiting cases, while its phase still changes.
