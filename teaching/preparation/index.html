PREREQUISITES & LEARNING RESOURCES

Refresh what you need.
Then return to the optics.

For learners with undergraduate calculus, linear algebra, and basic Python. Each lesson names the specific ideas it uses and links to focused refreshers. Review unfamiliar topics as needed; the lesson develops the optical method.

These resources are selected for a specific skill. Start with the indicated section or lecture summary; a whole textbook or course is not assigned. Use each lesson's checklist to decide what needs review.

For browser experiments: no Python setup is needed. For notebooks: be comfortable with Python functions, NumPy arrays, and reading plots.

Complex amplitudes and phase

You are ready when you can: Read A exp(iφ), distinguish amplitude from phase, and compute |u|² = u u*.

  • MIT OCW · Complex Arithmetic and Exponentials ↗

    Read the notes on Euler's formula, polar form, multiplication, and complex conjugation. The differential-equation applications can wait.

Used in E01, E02, E03, E04, E05, E06, E07, E08

Fourier transforms and convolution

You are ready when you can: Interpret Fourier coefficients as weights of spatial oscillations; relate convolution to multiplication in frequency space.

  • TU Delft · Fourier analysis ↗

    Use §4.1 for the transform pair, then §4.2.1–4.2.4 for linearity, scaling, shifts, and convolution. Consult §4.2.7 for two successive transforms.

Used in E01, E02, E03, E05, E10
For the deeper sections of E07

Matrices, eigenvectors, and modes

You are ready when you can: Multiply a matrix by a vector and explain why an eigenvector retains its direction.

  • MIT OCW · Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors ↗

    Start with the lecture summary and one 2 × 2 worked problem. Focus on Av = λv and what changes when a matrix is applied repeatedly.

Used in E01, E07, E08
For the deeper sections of E04

Complex inner products

You are ready when you can: Use x†y as a complex inner product and distinguish conjugate transpose from ordinary transpose.

  • MIT OCW · Lecture 26: Complex matrices; fast Fourier transform ↗

    Focus on complex vectors, conjugate transpose, Hermitian matrices, and unitary matrices. The FFT algorithm at the end is optional here.

Used in E10
For the deeper sections of E04

Averages, variance, and correlation

You are ready when you can: Distinguish an average of products from a product of averages, and recognize independent samples.

  • MIT OCW · Probability readings, Classes 4, 6, and 7 ↗

    Read Class 4b on expectation, Class 6a on expectation and variance, and Class 7b on covariance and correlation. R exercises are not needed.

Used in E04

A lens as a phase element

You are ready when you can: Describe how multiplying a field by exp(iφ(x,y)) changes phase while preserving its local intensity.

  • TU Delft · Coherent optical imaging ↗

    In §7.1, find the thin-lens transmission t_lens and the field immediately after the lens. Focus on multiplication by a position-dependent phase.

Used in E06

Geometric series

You are ready when you can: Sum repeated contributions whose amplitudes differ by the same factor each time.

  • OpenStax · Calculus Volume 2, §5.2 ↗

    Read the Geometric Series subsection: partial sums, the sum 1/(1−r), and the condition |r| < 1. Other series tests are not needed.

Used in E08

Coupled electric and magnetic fields

You are ready when you can: Explain why a changing electric field and a changing magnetic field must be evolved together.

  • OpenStax · University Physics Volume 2, §16.1 ↗

    Read Maxwell's Equations and the discussion connecting changing electric and magnetic fields. Concentrate on Faraday's and Ampère–Maxwell's laws.

Used in E09

Derivatives on a sampled grid

You are ready when you can: Read a difference quotient as an approximate derivative and distinguish grid spacing from time-step size.

  • MIT OCW · Lecture 10: Finite Differences in Time ↗

    Focus on replacing a derivative by a difference quotient and taking successive Euler steps. The stability discussion gives useful context for E09.

Used in E09

Chain rule and gradients

You are ready when you can: Differentiate a scalar objective through intermediate variables and interpret a gradient as sensitivity to parameters.

  • MIT OCW · Chain Rule, Gradient and Directional Derivatives ↗

    Use Sessions 32 and 34 for the multivariable chain rule, Session 35 for the gradient, and Session 38 for directional derivatives.

Used in E10

NumPy arrays for the labs

You are ready when you can: Create and slice an array, apply an elementwise function, and take a sum along an axis.

  • NumPy · The absolute basics for beginners ↗

    Use the sections on array shape, indexing and slicing, basic operations, and broadcasting. Practice changing one parameter and rerunning a cell.

FFT conventions in code

You are ready when you can: Distinguish sample coordinates from frequency bins and keep the forward/inverse normalization consistent.

  • NumPy · Discrete Fourier Transform ↗

    Read Implementation details and Normalization; look up fftfreq, fftshift, and ifftshift as they appear in the notebook.
